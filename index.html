From PSLE to SEC, step by step

Try your child's PSLE results and see the subject levels they could start with, how levels can change, and which pathways open after the SEC exams. Every choice you make here carries into the next step.

This is an illustration, not an MOE tool. Schools set their own timing and criteria within MOE's framework, so always confirm with your child's school.

Step 1 of 5

PSLE results to posting group

Choose the PSLE Achievement Level (AL) for each subject. The four ALs add up to the PSLE score, which sets the posting group.

PSLE score0

Posting groups (PG1, PG2, PG3) are used only to get into secondary school and to guide starting subject levels. They do not decide the pathway. This page uses Standard-level ALs. If your child sat Foundation-level subjects, ask the school about the options.

Step 2 of 5

Subject levels at the start of Sec 1

Most subjects start at the level for the posting group. Your child's ALs may allow some subjects at a more demanding level (MDL) or less demanding level (LDL). Pick the starting level for each subject.

Step 3 of 5

Moving between levels in lower secondary

Levels are not fixed. In school-based results, 75% or more in a subject typically gives the option to offer it at the next more demanding level from the following semester. Move the slider to try a result.

Moving up is the family's choice, and schools set the timing. Some schools ask students to stay with a more demanding subject until the end of Sec 2. Moving down is decided on performance and a holistic review, with no fixed mark. The try-lower button only shows what a lower level would mean for later steps. Students can move back up later.

Step 4 of 5

Subjects in upper secondary

Students take English, Mother Tongue, Humanities, Mathematics and Science, plus electives. Each is offered at the level set in step 3. Add up to two electives and choose their levels.

Electives include Computing (offered at G1, G2 and G3), Additional Mathematics, Art and Design and Technology. Which electives and levels are available depends on the school. Students taking mainly G1 subjects must take Computing and/or Science. Eligible students can offer Higher Mother Tongue instead of Mother Tongue.
